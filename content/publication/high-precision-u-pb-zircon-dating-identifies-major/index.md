title: "High-precision U–Pb zircon dating identifies a major magmatic event on the Moon at 4.338 Ga"
date: 2024-07-26
authors: ["Mélanie Barboni", "Dawid Szymanowski", "Blair Schoene", "Nicolas Dauphas", "Zhe J. Zhang", "Xi Chen", "Kevin D. McKeegan"]
publication_types: ["journal_article"]
publication: "Science Advances 10(30), eadn9871"
doi: "10.1126/sciadv.adn9871"
abstract: "The Moon has had a complex history, with evidence of its primary crust formation obscured by later impacts. Existing U-Pb dates of >500 zircons from several locations on the lunar nearside reveal a pronounced age peak at 4.33 billion years (Ga), suggesting a major, potentially global magmatic event. However, the precision of existing geochronology is insufficient to determine whether this peak represents a brief event or a more protracted period of magmatism occurring over tens of millions of years. To improve the temporal resolution, we have analyzed Apollo 14, 15, and 17 zircons that were previously dated by ion microprobe at ~4.33 Ga using isotope dilution thermal ionization mass spectrometry. Concordant dates with sub-million-year uncertainty span ~4 million years from 4.338 to 4.334 Ga. Combined with Hf isotopic ratios and trace element concentrations, the data suggest zircon formation in a large impact melt sheet, possibly linked to the South Pole–Aitken basin."
