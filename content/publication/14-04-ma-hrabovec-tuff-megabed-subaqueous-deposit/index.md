abstract: "The East Slovak Basin forms part of the Miocene Pannonian back-arc Basin System developed in response to extension above a retreating subducted slab at the Alpine–Carpathian–Dinaridic junction. Within this basin, the Hrabovec Tuff represents a 100–140 m thick zeolitized rhyolite/dacite tuff “megabed” at the top of the lower Badenian (Langhian) strata. The originally vitric tuff, composed of glass shards, plagioclase, and biotite with rare sanidine and quartz crystals, underwent alteration in which the glass shards were replaced by various zeolite minerals and opal/quartz depending on burial depth. LA-ICP-MS and high-precision CA–ID–TIMS U–Pb zircon age data, together with ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar biotite ages, document that the Hrabovec Tuff eruption occurred at 14.039 +0.019/−0.034 Ma. The uniformity of zircon geochemistry across multiple samples supports derivation from a single eruptive event. Grain-size characteristics, compositional variability, and the presence of marine fossils indicate deposition in a marine environment as a subaqueous eruption-fed density current. The most conservative volume estimate, based on seismic interpretation and restricted to the study area, yields approximately 22 km³. Modelling results from the Trhovište-26 well suggest a decompacted thickness of about 211 m, increasing the total erupted volume within the study area to an estimated 39 km³. Thus, the Hrabovec Tuff represents an additional major eruptive event in the Middle Miocene volcanic history and warrants incorporation into the tephrostratigraphy of the Carpathian–Pannonian region."
